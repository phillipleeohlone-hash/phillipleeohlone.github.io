Step 1 · Center

Mean, Median, and Skew

  • The mean is the balance point of a distribution. The median is the middle value.
  • In a symmetric distribution the two are about equal.
  • A long tail on one side pulls the mean toward that side. In a right-skewed distribution (long right tail) the mean tends to be larger than the median. In a left-skewed one it tends to be smaller.
  • The mean is affected by extreme values. The median is much less so.

Steps 2–3 · New Method: np.std

The SD and np.std

  • A deviation is a value minus the mean. Deviations always add up to zero, so we square them first.
  • The average of the squared deviations is the variance. Its square root is the SD, also called the root mean square of the deviations.
  • The SD describes, roughly, how far values tend to be from the mean. A larger SD means more spread.
  • np.std(arr) does all of that in one call. Use it in this course. The pandas method .std() divides by one fewer and gives a slightly different answer on small data.
Example with a made-up array
values = np.array([1, 3, 5, 7])

np.mean(values)
# 4.0

np.std(values)
# 2.236...

The Normal Curve
How much of the data lies near the mean. Step 4.
Step 4 · Picture

The Normal Curve and the SD

  • A normal curve is symmetric and bell-shaped, centered at the mean.
  • The SD sets its scale. The red dots are the points of inflection, where the curve changes from bending downward to bending upward. Each is about one SD from the mean.
  • The darker band is within 1 SD of the mean. The lighter band is within 2 SDs.
Step 4 · Counting Within an SD

Fraction of Data Within k SDs

  • For a bell-shaped (normal) distribution, the empirical rule gives known fractions of values within 1, 2 and 3 SDs of the mean (see Chapter 14.3).
  • A value is within k SDs of the mean if np.abs(value - mean) is at most k * sd.
  • Comparing an array to a number gives True/False for each value. np.mean of that array is the fraction of Trues.
  • The empirical rule describes bell-shaped data, not skewed data. Chebyshev's bound holds for any distribution but is weaker: at least 75% of values are within 2 SDs.
Example with made-up data and 3 SDs
np.abs(scores - np.mean(scores)) <= 3 * np.std(scores)
# an array of True / False

np.mean(np.abs(scores - np.mean(scores))
        <= 3 * np.std(scores))
# the fraction within 3 SDs

Step 5 · New Method: table.sample

Sampling Rows from a Table

  • table.sample(n, replace=True) draws n whole rows at random from a DataFrame, putting each row back after it is drawn.
  • The result is a smaller table. Pick one column with ['col'], then summarize it.
  • It works like the Series version you used for the bootstrap, but the rows stay together.
  • Writing the table, column and size as arguments lets one function work on any table.
Example with a made-up table
shop.sample(3, replace=True)
# 3 rows; a row can appear twice

shop.sample(3, replace=True)['sales'].median()
# the median of 'sales' in one sample

Patterns
What happens to the sample means as the sample size changes. Steps 7–9.
Steps 6–9 · The Idea

The Central Limit Theorem

  • The distribution of the sums or means of large random samples, drawn with replacement from a population, is roughly normal.
  • This holds whatever the shape of the population.
  • It describes the distribution of a statistic (the sample mean), not the distribution of the original data.
  • How a statistic varies across many repeated samples is its sampling distribution. Larger samples give sample means that are more tightly clustered around the population mean.
  • The sampling distribution of the sample mean is centered near the population mean, so the sample mean is an unbiased estimator.
Steps 5–8 · Picture

Three Different Distributions

All three are drawn on the same scale. The red line is the population mean.

1. The population
Every value. Wide and skewed. Its SD is the population SD.
2. One sample
A random sample of values. A rough copy of the population, with a similar spread.
3. Many sample means
One mean from each of many samples. This is the sampling distribution of the sample mean, and it is much narrower.
  • The population SD describes how spread out the individual values are.
  • The SD of the sample means, called the standard error, describes how much sample means vary from sample to sample. It is a different number.
